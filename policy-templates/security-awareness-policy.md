# Security Awareness and Training Policy

**Owner:** [Owner Role — typically Head of People / HR or CISO]
**Effective Date:** [Effective Date]
**Next Review:** [Effective Date + 1 year]
**Version:** 1.0

## Purpose

This policy establishes [Company Name]'s commitment to a continuous, role-appropriate program of security awareness and training. Most security failures begin with a human action — a misclicked link, an over-shared file, a missed configuration — and the most reliable way to reduce them is a workforce that recognizes threats and knows how to respond.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and other personnel with access to [Company Name]'s systems or data. Role-specific provisions apply additionally to engineers, administrators, leadership, and others handling elevated risk.

## Policy statements

### 1. Program objectives

1.1. The security awareness program shall:
- Equip every person with the knowledge to handle company information securely
- Build recognition of common attack patterns relevant to [Company Name]'s threat landscape
- Reinforce expectations from the [Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable-use-policy.md) and other policies
- Provide role-specific depth for personnel with elevated access or responsibilities
- Measure effectiveness and adapt content based on outcomes

### 2. New hire training

2.1. All new employees and contractors shall complete the security awareness module within their first thirty (30) days, and before being granted access to Confidential or Restricted data.

2.2. The new-hire module shall cover, at minimum:
- Overview of [Company Name]'s information security program
- Acceptable use, including data handling
- Authentication and credential hygiene
- Phishing and social engineering recognition
- Device security expectations
- Incident and concern reporting channels
- Privacy expectations
- Code of conduct and reporting of unethical behavior

2.3. Completion shall be tracked and acknowledged in writing. The acknowledgement record shall include the policies the individual has reviewed.

### 3. Annual refresher

3.1. All personnel shall complete refresher training at least annually. Refresher modules shall update content to reflect current threats and lessons from incidents during the prior year.

3.2. Completion shall be tracked centrally. Non-completion within thirty (30) days of the deadline shall be escalated to the individual's manager.

### 4. Role-specific training

4.1. **Engineers** shall complete secure coding training at least annually. The curriculum shall cover, at minimum: OWASP Top 10 categories relevant to [Company Name]'s tech stack, authentication and authorization patterns, secrets management, dependency hygiene, and platform-specific risks (cloud configurations, container security).

4.2. **Personnel with administrative or privileged access** shall complete additional training on operational security, change management discipline, and incident response procedures.

4.3. **Personnel handling personal data** shall complete privacy-focused training covering applicable regulations and [Company Name]'s privacy program.

4.4. **Leadership** shall complete training appropriate to their decision-making responsibilities (risk governance, incident escalation, breach notification).

4.5. **Customer-support personnel and others handling customer data inquiries** shall complete training on verification of identity, data-subject rights handling, and escalation paths.

### 5. Phishing simulations

5.1. [Company Name] shall conduct simulated phishing exercises at least quarterly. Scenarios shall reflect current threat patterns and progressively test more sophisticated attacks.

5.2. Results — campaign reach, click rate, report rate — shall be tracked centrally and reported to leadership at least quarterly.

5.3. Individuals who click simulated phishes shall be enrolled in targeted refresher training. Repeated falls within a defined period shall trigger manager involvement and tailored coaching, not disciplinary action by default — the program is designed to teach, not punish.

5.4. Reporting suspected phishing (real or simulated) shall be recognized positively. Personnel who report quickly help the entire organization respond to active threats.

### 6. Ongoing communication

6.1. Beyond formal training, the [Owner Role] shall communicate periodic security updates — at minimum, on the cadence of significant new threats, control changes, or policy updates.

6.2. Channels may include all-hands updates, written communications, intranet content, and posters or signage where appropriate. Content shall be concise and actionable; lengthy or jargon-heavy notices generally produce no behavior change.

6.3. Lessons learned from incidents and near-misses shall be communicated in anonymized form across the organization to reinforce the value of reporting and the realism of threats.

### 7. Specialized training events

7.1. **Tabletop exercises** — incident response tabletops are conducted at least annually under the [Incident Response Policy](incident-response-policy.md) and serve as practical training for response teams.

7.2. **Privacy and regulatory updates** — when applicable laws change materially (e.g., a new state privacy law, GDPR amendment), affected personnel shall receive targeted updates.

### 8. Vendor-provided training

8.1. Personnel using specialized tools provided by third parties (security tooling, cloud-native services, compliance platforms) shall complete vendor-provided training as needed to operate the tool effectively.

### 9. Measuring effectiveness

9.1. The [Owner Role] shall measure the awareness program through:
- Completion rates for required training
- Phishing simulation outcomes (click rate, report rate, time-to-report)
- Number and quality of suspected-phish reports from personnel
- Frequency of policy-violation incidents attributable to awareness gaps
- Feedback collected from training participants

9.2. Metrics shall be reported to leadership at least quarterly, with adjustments to content or cadence based on results.

### 10. Record retention

10.1. Training records — including completion of new-hire training, annual refreshers, role-specific training, and phishing simulation outcomes — shall be retained for at least two (2) years post-completion or the duration of employment plus one year, whichever is longer.

10.2. Records shall be made available for audit sampling.

### 11. Updates to content

11.1. Training content shall be reviewed at least annually and updated to reflect:
- New or modified policies
- Lessons learned from incidents
- Changes in the threat landscape
- Changes in the technology stack
- Customer or regulatory feedback

### 12. Special obligations

12.1. Personnel handling specifically regulated data (payment card, health information, certain personal-data categories) shall complete training required by the applicable regulation. The [Owner Role] in consultation with Legal Counsel determines applicability.
