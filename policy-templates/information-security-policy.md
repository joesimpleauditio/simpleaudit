## Purpose

This policy establishes [Company Name]'s overall approach to information security. It defines the principles, responsibilities, and high-level requirements that govern the protection of the company's information assets and the information entrusted to the company by its customers, employees, and partners. All other security policies in [Company Name]'s policy library derive from and operate under this Information Security Policy.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, consultants, temporary staff, and any third party with access to [Company Name]'s information systems or data. It covers all information assets — regardless of format (electronic, paper, verbal) or location (cloud, office, remote workplace) — that are owned, processed, or stored by [Company Name], including information owned by customers and entrusted to [Company Name].

## Policy statements

### 1. Information security objectives

[Company Name] shall maintain an information security program designed to:

1.1. Preserve the **confidentiality** of customer, employee, and corporate information by preventing unauthorized disclosure.

1.2. Preserve the **integrity** of information by preventing unauthorized or accidental modification or destruction.

1.3. Preserve the **availability** of information and information systems to authorized users when needed to support business operations.

1.4. Comply with applicable laws, regulations, and contractual obligations relating to information security and privacy.

1.5. Manage information security risk to a level acceptable to [Company Name]'s leadership.

### 2. Governance

2.1. The [Owner Role] is the executive owner of the information security program and is accountable to leadership for its effectiveness.

2.2. Information security responsibilities, oversight, and decision-making authority shall be documented and communicated to relevant personnel.

2.3. Information security risks shall be reviewed by leadership at least annually, with material changes communicated promptly.

2.4. The information security program shall be aligned with the AICPA Trust Services Criteria for systems within audit scope.

### 3. Policy framework

3.1. The Information Security Policy is supported by a set of topic-specific policies addressing access control, acceptable use, risk management, vendor management, incident response, change management, vulnerability management, security awareness, and other domains as required.

3.2. All policies shall be reviewed at least annually by the [Owner Role] or designated owner, and approved by leadership.

3.3. Policies shall be made available to all personnel through [policy location — e.g., the company intranet, HR platform, or shared drive].

3.4. Material updates to any policy require communication to affected personnel within thirty (30) days of approval.

### 4. Roles and accountability

4.1. **Leadership** is accountable for setting the security program's direction, approving policies, and providing the resources needed to maintain the program.

4.2. **The [Owner Role]** is responsible for the day-to-day management of the program, including policy maintenance, risk assessment, and reporting to leadership.

4.3. **Managers** are responsible for ensuring that their teams understand and comply with applicable policies.

4.4. **All personnel** are responsible for following the policies, completing required training, and reporting suspected security incidents, policy violations, or vulnerabilities through approved channels.

### 5. Information classification and handling

5.1. Information shall be classified according to the [Data Classification Policy](data-classification-policy.md). Handling requirements — including access, transmission, storage, and disposal — derive from the classification.

5.2. Customer data is, by default, classified at the most sensitive level used by [Company Name] and shall be handled accordingly.

### 6. Risk management

6.1. [Company Name] shall maintain a documented risk management process as defined in the [Risk Management Policy](risk-management-policy.md).

6.2. Significant changes to the business, technology stack, or threat landscape shall trigger reassessment of relevant risks.

### 7. Compliance and exceptions

7.1. Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract, and may be referred to law enforcement where applicable.

7.2. Exceptions to any provision of this policy require written approval from the [Owner Role] and shall be documented with a stated business justification, compensating controls, and an expiration date no later than twelve (12) months from approval.

7.3. The [Owner Role] shall review open exceptions at least quarterly.

### 8. Monitoring and continuous improvement

8.1. The effectiveness of the information security program shall be measured through key risk indicators, audit results, incident metrics, and assessment outcomes.

8.2. Findings from audits, assessments, and incidents shall be tracked to remediation, with progress reported to leadership at least quarterly.
